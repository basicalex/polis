# Prime review — polis compression plan

## Status per point

### 1. Canonical golden path — DRIFTING
`README.md` leads with governance mapping, claims, office-holders, and document verification rather than the agreed resident-to-public-record sequence. The homepage starts after complaint filing and offers only “Inspect process → Verify record → Read transparency” (`apps/web/src/pages/index.astro`), omitting acknowledgment, official response, evidence, independent review, and a unified public record. `ROADMAP.md` defines another flagship workflow beginning with an accepted charter, while complaints, deliberation, and commitments remain competing flows across `apps/web/src/pages/complaints/`, `apps/web/src/pages/issues/`, and `apps/web/src/pages/commitments/`.

### 2. One case object — STALLED
The schema keeps complaints, claims, evidence links, commitments, status events, reviews, and audit events as separate structures without a shared public accountability-case identifier (`packages/db/src/schema.ts`). The private complaint detail does render a “Case timeline,” but it covers complaint requests, decisions, and appeal—not commitments, proofs, or independent public review (`apps/web/src/pages/complaints/[id].astro`). Claims and commitments still have separate detail pages (`apps/web/src/pages/claims/[id].astro`, `apps/web/src/pages/commitments/[id].astro`), so there is no unified public case UI.

### 3. Navigation ~4 items — DRIFTING
The resident primary navigation has 13 permanent links plus authentication: 14 links signed out and 15 signed in, rather than roughly four (`apps/web/src/layouts/Base.astro`). Audit, Verify, Proofs, Methodology, and Transparency are all promoted to top-level navigation, directly contradicting the requirement to place trust affordances in context.

### 4. DESIGN.md as contract — STALLED
`DESIGN.md` remains mostly an unfilled template: audience, promise, personality, visual principles, layout, component rules, content design, and accessibility fields are blank. The web app implements its dark tokens, monospace typography, responsive grids, and a mobile header rule (`apps/web/src/styles.css`, `apps/web/src/layouts/Base.astro`, `packages/ui/src/styles/base.css`). However, the document does not define the promised calm, minimal resident experience, and the crowded navigation does not demonstrate one.

### 5. Service count (parked) — STALLED, 18 backend services (22 including apps)
The canonical catalog contains 18 first-party backend services (`config/services.json`), matching the service table and “17 Node plus Python” statement in `README.md`. Adding the four Astro apps listed in `README.md` gives 22 first-party app/service deployables; `infra/compose/docker-compose.yml` adds infrastructure and debug-profile entries beyond those. Consolidation below 18 backend services has not occurred.

### 6. Filing status contradiction — DRIFTING
Commitment filing currently inserts an approved claim, an approved submission, and a public commitment with an initial proposed lifecycle event (`services/contribution-service/src/representative-routes.ts`); tests explicitly assert the submission is approved (`services/contribution-service/src/index.test.ts`). In contrast, the admin drafting surface says commitments are pending review, and the older acceptance contract expects a pending submission followed by reviewer approval (`apps/admin/src/pages/drafting.astro`, `scripts/phase-mra2-acceptance.mjs`). No consistent filing status holds repo-wide.

## Drift flags

- **New surface area:** Deliberation issues, rewards, contributions, assistant pages, a separate verifier, and a citizen vault remain independent product surfaces (`apps/web/src/pages/issues.astro`, `apps/web/src/pages/rewards.astro`, `apps/web/src/pages/contribute/`, `apps/web/src/pages/assistant.astro`, `apps/verifier/`, `apps/vault/`). They widen the product instead of composing into one resident accountability case.
- **New pillars:** Audit, verification, proofs, methodology, transparency, and privacy occupy primary resident navigation and homepage resource space (`apps/web/src/layouts/Base.astro`, `apps/web/src/pages/index.astro`). These trust mechanisms are presented as destinations rather than contextual receipts.
- **Story divergence:** `README.md` demonstrates governance → claim → office-holder → document verification; the homepage demonstrates complaint-process inspection → verification → transparency; `ROADMAP.md` prioritizes charter → proof → commitment → review; and admin/verifier homepages advertise six generic infrastructure capabilities (`apps/admin/src/pages/index.astro`, `apps/verifier/src/pages/index.astro`). The surfaces do not tell one golden-path story.

## Top risks

1. **No shared product narrative:** The conflicting paths in `README.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, and `apps/web/src/pages/index.astro` threaten the central compression goal and make it unclear what residents should do first.
2. **No public case projection:** Separate complaint, claim, commitment, proof, review, and audit models/pages (`packages/db/src/schema.ts`, `apps/web/src/pages/`) threaten the promise that a resident can follow one record with receipts.
3. **Contracts cannot be trusted:** Approved-versus-pending commitment behavior disagrees across implementation, UI, and acceptance scripts (`services/contribution-service/src/representative-routes.ts`, `apps/admin/src/pages/drafting.astro`, `scripts/phase-mra2-acceptance.mjs`), threatening both review semantics and executable validation.
